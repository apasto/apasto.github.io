Using an RTLSDR to log GSM900 whole-band RMS power

April 22, 2015

A 4-day long power log of the GSM900 downlink. Black: raw data points, red: 1 hour moving average, blue: after LP filter at 12 hours.

Summary: I measured the average power (in non-calibrated dBFS) across the whole GSM900 band, using an rtl-sdr, rtl_power and this awk script from Kyle Keen (2026 edit: archived link here). The power shows a daily and sub-daily periodicity, which I assume is mostly due to variation in cell traffic trough time (to which the protocol duty cycle is proportional).

One of the most interesting aspects that emerges from capturing and plotting a day-long waterfall plot (TODO: a post from 2013, that I have yet to migrate to this blog) is periodicity over time of the power spectral density in certain bands, which is more noticeable in the GSM900 downlink (from the cell base station to the phone) than anywhere else. This useful awk one-liner (credits) makes it possible:

awk 'BEGIN {t=0} {if (t==0) {t=$2; n=0; s=0}; if (t==$2) {s+=$7; n++} else {print s/n; t=0}}'

which in practice integrates into a pipe from rtl_power like this:

rtl_power -f 925M:965M:2M -g 46 -i 5 -p -21 |
awk 'BEGIN {t=0} {if (t==0) {t=$2; n=0; s=0}; if (t==$2) {s+=$7; n++} else {print s/int s/n; t=0; fflush()}}' |
cat >> $LOGFILE

Zoom-in on 36 hours of recording, covering a Thursday and part of a Friday"

At this point, writing this post (started at the end of October) got bogged down in trying to make the conjectures “robust” enough to provide a plausible explanation for the phenomenon, or at least enough to make them decent for this blog. In short: I think we are seeing the superimposed effects of propagation, distance, and traffic, and some other lesser order effects.

To which can we attribute most of the variations seen here to?

Propagation is affected by water absorption (so-called rain fade), which is minimal at 900 MHz, so I deem it close to negligible; for distance I wrongly tought that the base station could be putting out higher power to reach farther devices - this seemed plausible, but in GSM that is a capability of the mobile terminal only). Then traffic. That is: channel congestion (density of packets in time?). Indeed, the protocol’s duty cycle approaches 100% as the channel gets closer to saturation. In support of this, I can only cite this comment from Reddit r/rtlsdr, as I must admit I have neither the desire, time, nor skills to dive into primary sources right now.
