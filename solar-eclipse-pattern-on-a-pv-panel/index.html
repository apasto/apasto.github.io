Solar eclipse pattern on a PV panel

August 28, 2026

I happen to have deployed a solar panel at a remote site, a feat I plan to write about in detail eventually. Before I get around to writing a proper post serie covering the story, let me just tell you that it feels like having my very own little satellite that could (sturdily guyed to the ground!).

So, my panel has experienced a partial solar eclipse: the Solar eclipse of August 12, 2026. This of course does not make it special in any way :)

There (roughly N45 E13) the eclipse more or less coincided with the sunset (20:19 CEST), so I wondered if the pattern of PV voltage through time differed from that of the surrounding days.

Here it is:

The overall sunset trend is clearly visible, with some interesting features and some weather variability. The eclipse seems to have significantly changed the sunset and twilight voltage pattern. Note that the sun set behind clouds, so there was significant scattering. Unfortunately, my only IP camera on-site at the time was pointing east.

There is also a peculiar systematic step before each sunset, followed by a plateau and that twilight drop-off ramp. Note that at this time of the year the sun is setting behind the south-facing PV panel, so some local effect (shadows and/or backscattering) might be involved.
